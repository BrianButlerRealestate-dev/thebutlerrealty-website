Historic Downtown Florence

Florence neighborhood guide • Homes, schools, daily life, and the tradeoffs

Neighborhoods/Florence/Historic Downtown Florence

The short version

Downtown Florence is the original town, and it does not look or live like the subdivisions north of it. Main Street runs past the second Pinal County Courthouse from 1891, McFarland State Historic Park in the first courthouse from 1878, the Florence Community Library, town hall, and a small set of local restaurants and shops. Around it are the town's oldest neighborhoods: historic adobe and territorial-era homes in and near the historic district, post-war and later homes in the original town subdivisions, and — a few streets away — established manufactured home areas including Florence Gardens.

People buy in historic Florence because they want to live in a town with a past they can walk through, and because the entry price for a home in town can be lower than the master plans. They give up new construction, big amenities, and in some streets, the certainty that every neighboring home is maintained the same way an HOA would require.

Buying a historic or older in-town home — what changes

  • Systems and structure diligence goes up. Adobe and older construction need an inspector who knows them: roof structure and drainage, foundation movement, older plumbing and electrical, and evaporative-to-refrigerated air conversions. Budget for the inspection and for the work it finds. None of it is a reason to avoid these homes. All of it is a reason not to price one like a 2015 subdivision home.
  • Historic district rules. If the home is in the historic district or is a contributing property, exterior changes can require review. Confirm with the Town of Florence before you plan an addition, a wall, or a roofline change.
  • Insurance and lending. Older homes and manufactured homes can carry different insurance costs and loan conditions. Quote both before you are in escrow.
  • Land and lots. In-town Florence has vacant lots and small parcels that come up near the subdivisions. Check zoning, flood plain, utility taps (water and sewer at the lot versus a tap fee away), and whether the street is town-maintained.

Daily life

You can walk more of your life here than anywhere else in Florence: library, town hall, courthouse events, Main Street restaurants, the senior center. The Dorothy Nolan Senior Center runs daily activities, a Wednesday biscuits and gravy breakfast, and Friday home-style lunches that are open community fixtures, not just senior programming. Coolidge, about 15 minutes south, handles the larger grocery and Walmart runs. Queen Creek and San Tan Valley are north on SR-79 and Hunt Highway when you need them.

What you will not find: a master plan pool, a golf course in the neighborhood, or a builder model to compare against. In-town Florence is for people who prefer a town to a community amenity package.

Who Historic Downtown Florence Fits

Good fit

Buyers who want character, history, and a walkable town core; buyers priced out of the master plans who would rather put money into an older home they can improve over time; county and town employees who want a short commute; sellers of well-kept in-town homes, because renovated historic and in-town homes are scarce compared to subdivision resales.

Look elsewhere if

You want new construction and a warranty (the corridor west and north of town), a clubhouse and golf lifestyle (Merrill Ranch), or strict HOA-maintained uniformity on every street.

Common Questions About Historic Downtown Florence

Are there rules on changing a historic home's exterior?

If the property is in the historic district, exterior work can require town review. Confirm the property's status and the rules with the Town of Florence before planning changes.

Is downtown Florence in an HOA?

Many in-town homes are not in an HOA. Verify for the specific property, and if no HOA matters to you, that freedom also means no HOA enforcement on neighboring properties.

What is McFarland State Historic Park?

The preserved first Pinal County Courthouse from the territorial period at Main and Ruggles, now a state historic park with the restored courtroom and exhibits. It is the front door to Florence's history and the starting point for walking tours of the historic district.
